# Scaffolding a Series · 系列性課程的連貫與鷹架

Use this for **series-based (系列性)** lessons only. A standalone (單元性) lesson goes straight into its topic and doesn't need any of this.

## Step 1: plan the series before any lesson
A series is designed top-down. Before designing an individual lesson, the designer settles the **series map 系列大綱** (`templates/series-map.md`):
- How many lessons in total, and the topic of each
- Their order, and whether Advanced/Basic tracks run in parallel
- What each lesson adds: new vocabulary, sentence patterns, concepts
- How difficulty rises across the series
- The thread that runs through it (a story, metaphor or question), if any
- Where any follow-up game or activity sits

Draft it as versions (v0.1, v0.2…) and get the designer's sign-off. Only then design lessons one by one, each against its slot in the map. If the map changes later, check the lessons already designed still fit.

## Step 2: before designing a lesson, map what kids already have
"Already learned" comes from two places — use both:
1. **School baseline 學校程度** — what kids at this grade have met at school: the MOE 1000/1200 word lists, the GEPT Kids list and the publicly available 翰林／南一／康軒 elementary English textbooks (plus any list or textbook the user adds). This applies to every lesson, series or not.
2. **Earlier lessons in this series 系列前面的課** — what the series map says earlier lessons taught on top of the school baseline.

From these, list:
- Vocabulary already taught
- Sentence patterns already taught
- Concepts / big ideas already taught
- The metaphor or story the series uses, if any

Put this list in the outline under 「已學過的單字／句型／概念」. Everything new in this lesson should connect to something on it.

## While designing: build one step up
- **Reuse, then extend.** Bring back 2–3 earlier words or the earlier sentence pattern, then add the new ones on top (e.g. a pattern learned as single words last time becomes a full sentence this time).
- **One step of difficulty.** Raise only one thing at a time — more words, *or* a longer sentence, *or* a harder concept — not all at once.
- **Keep the thread.** If the series has a carrying metaphor, story or character, keep using it so kids recognise where they are.
- **Review bridge at the start.** 1–2 slides bringing back the previous lesson, ideally as a question kids answer ("Last time we learned … — who remembers?").
- **Set up what comes next.** Make sure the words and ideas the next lesson needs are taught here. Whether to preview the next lesson in the wrap-up is the designer's call.
- **Don't re-teach.** Earlier content gets a quick review, not a full lesson — the time goes to new content and practice.

## Basic/Advanced inside a series
Each level scaffolds along its own track: a Basic lesson builds on the previous *Basic* lesson. Check that vocabulary and difficulty step up consistently within each track.

## Check
- [ ] Prior-knowledge list filled in from earlier lessons
- [ ] New words/patterns connect to earlier ones
- [ ] Difficulty rises by one step only
- [ ] Review bridge at the start
- [ ] What the next lesson needs is taught here
- [ ] No full re-teaching of earlier content
